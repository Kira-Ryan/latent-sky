Project concept · at a glance

Latent Sky

AI WEATHER · RENDERED AS A LIVING GLOBE

An interactive 3D globe that shows you the weather as an AI predicts it.

It pulls real atmospheric data, runs it through NVIDIA's open Earth‑2 forecasting models, and paints the result onto a planet you can turn, zoom and scrub through time.

Its signature moment: you fly down to a region and watch a blurry global forecast snap into razor‑sharp kilometre‑scale detail — invented by the AI, not measured.

How it works

The whole trick is the split: the expensive part happens once, offline. The part your visitor touches is featherweight.

Offline — happens once, on a rented GPU The AI wow — coarse becomes sharp Live — runs on the visitor's laptop

What someone actually experiences

Six moments, in order, from opening the link to sending it to a friend.

Moment one

It's already alive

No loading screen, no settings, no empty state. The page opens on a dark Earth already turning, with weather already moving across it.

Moment two

Weather you can read

One atmospheric field painted across the planet in honest, perceptually even colour — wind ribbons, cloud bands — with a legend that always says what the colours mean.

Moment three

They scrub the future

A timeline along the bottom. Press play and days of predicted weather flow past smoothly; grab the scrubber and move through the forecast by hand, forwards or back.

Moment four — the reveal

Blurry becomes sharp

They fly down to a region. Terrain rises. The soft gradient resolves into real structure — storm cells, rain bands with edges — generated by the AI, not measured. This is the moment the project exists for.

Moment five

They play

Switch variables, orbit the region, go back out to the whole planet, read a short panel on which models made this and where the data came from. Few controls, all quiet, none in the way.

Moment six

They send it on

They leave understanding what AI weather models actually do — and with an impression of whoever built it. The repo and a 90-second video sit quietly at the bottom for anyone who wants more.

The whole design test: a meteorologist should find it accurate, and someone who knows nothing about weather should find it beautiful — and both of them should keep dragging the timeline.
