Placement skill by neighbourhood scale

Gridpoint scores punish a convection-allowing model twice for a storm that is slightly misplaced: a miss where it was, a false alarm where it went. The Fractions Skill Score compares the fraction of cells above a threshold inside a neighbourhood around every point, so it asks the fairer question: at what scale does the forecast put storms where they were?

Coverage: how much storm, and when

Coverage is the fraction of the domain above a threshold. It says nothing about placement, which is exactly why it is useful next to FSS: it separates too much or too little from in the wrong place.

Method

Forecast
NVIDIA StormCast v1 (Apache-2.0 checkpoint) via earth2studio 0.17.0. HRRR analysis initial conditions at 18Z, GFS_FX conditioning at every hourly step, one realisation, seed fixed.
Observation
NOAA MRMS MergedReflectivityQCComposite_00.50 from noaa-mrms-pds, the file nearest each top of the hour. Worst time offset 43 seconds.
Grid
Both fields on the same 884×661 equirectangular grid the site renders, 2.39 km per pixel, rebuilt with the encoder's own rule and checked against the shipped manifest. Forecast regridded nearest-neighbour from its 512×640 curvilinear grid; MRMS from 0.01° by nearest cell.
Masking
MRMS -999 (outside radar coverage) excluded; -99 (coverage, no echo) counted as 0 dBZ. Forecast pixels outside the curvilinear footprint excluded. The sentinel meaning was established empirically: zero -999 cells inside the domain, and the Gulf and Atlantic corners entirely -999.
FSS
Roberts and Lean 2008. Binary exceedance at 20, 30 and 40 dBZ; square neighbourhoods of 1, 5, 11, 21 and 41 pixels (2, 12, 26, 50 and 98 km); FSS = 1 − MSE(Pf, Po) / (mean Pf2 + mean Po2) over valid cells. Cells outside the mask are zero in both fields before filtering, so the mask edge neither creates nor absorbs neighbourhood mass. Self-tested: FSS(f, f) = 1, FSS(f, 0) = 0, non-decreasing with scale.
Caveats
One diffusion sample, not an ensemble. MRMS is radar-derived and quality-controlled, not absolute truth. Nearest-cell resampling of 1 km radar to 2.4 km keeps peaks but drops some texture. Zero-padding at the domain edge slightly deflates the largest neighbourhoods near the boundary.
